Three phases, gated by data

PHASE 1

Now

Uses existing data. No machine learning.
  • At-risk student detection
  • Session performance dashboard
  • Grade trajectory chart on the student portal
PHASE 2

After 2+ sessions

Needs enough history to compare.
  • Course difficulty index
  • Fee collection forecasting
  • Lecturer load analysis
  • Graduation likelihood scoring
PHASE 3

After 3+ years

Machine learning, only with real cohorts.
  • Dropout prediction
  • Personalised unit recommendations
  • Fee payment behaviour modelling
Phase 3 waits. An underfitted model is worse than no model — it creates false confidence. Dropout prediction needs at least three cohorts of graduated and dropped students before it is deployed.

Privacy & ethics

Analytics on student data carries real weight. These are the rules the design holds itself to, in line with the Kenya Data Protection Act.

Acceptable

  • Aggregate statistics where no individual is identified
  • At-risk scores visible only to authorised staff, department admin and above
  • Students seeing their own trajectory
  • Anonymised data for any model training
  • Flags built on documented, explainable signals that staff can review and override

Not acceptable

  • Sharing an individual's risk score publicly or with other students
  • Using ethnicity, religion or county as predictive features without an ethical review
  • Automated decisions based on model output alone — a person must review and act
  • Retaining raw student data past its retention period for analytics
Students have the right to know when automated processing informed a score or decision that affects them. See the student data rights.
